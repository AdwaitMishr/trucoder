Updating a Deployment with 10 replicas by replacing all 10 at once would be
briefly catastrophic — there is a moment with zero capacity. So Kubernetes
updates **incrementally**, and `maxUnavailable` is the speed limit on how fast
it can break things.

## The rule

`maxUnavailable` caps how many pods may be down during a rolling update. You
can write it as a number (`maxUnavailable: 1`) or as a **percentage** of the
replica count. Percentages round **down**.

:::example
10 replicas, `maxUnavailable: 25%`:

10 × 25 / 100 = 2.5 → floor → **2** pods may be down.

5 replicas, 50%: 5 × 50 / 100 = 2.5 → **2**.
:::

## Why rounding down matters

Kubernetes treats `maxUnavailable` as a hard cap on how many pods it may take
down at any step. Rounding down keeps you inside the budget — 2.5 pods is
nonsense, and the safe side is 2, not 3.

- 1 replica, 25%: 0.25 → floor → **0** — the update can only happen when a
  replacement is already ready (this is why production configs usually set it
  to at least 1, or use a percentage that yields 1+).
- 3 replicas, 100%: **3** — all pods may go down at once. Equivalent to a
  stop-the-world replace.

:::warning
Multiply **before** you divide. Integer division first (`25 / 100 = 0`) would
zero out every percentage below 100. Compute `replicas * pct`, then divide.
:::

:::tip
`maxSurge` is the mirror image: how many pods may exist *above* the desired
count during the update. Together they define the update's blast radius —
`maxUnavailable` is the down-side, `maxSurge` is the up-side.
:::
