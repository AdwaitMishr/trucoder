The Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) is a controller with a simple job: keep the
average utilization of your pods near a target, by adding or removing replicas.

## The formula

```
desiredReplicas = ceil(currentReplicas × currentUtilization / targetUtilization)
```

Read it as a proportion. If utilization is *double* the target, you need *twice*
the pods:

```
currentUtilization / targetUtilization = 80 / 50 = 1.6
4 replicas × 1.6 = 6.4  ->  ceil ->  7 replicas
```

:::example
- 4 replicas at 80% CPU, target 50%: 4 × 1.6 = 6.4 → **7**.
- 4 replicas at 50%, target 50%: ratio 1.0 → **4**. No change.
- 2 replicas at 30%, target 50%: 2 × 0.6 = 1.2 → ceil → 2 → **2**. (The ratio
  says 1.2 pods, but pods are indivisible — and the floor is 1.)
:::

## Two traps

**Rounding.** `ceil` matters. 6.4 pods means 7 — 6 pods would still be
overloaded. Always round up.

**The floor.** The HPA never scales a workload to zero replicas. Whatever the
math says, the answer is at least **1**. (That is also why "zero utilization"
still returns 1, not 0.)

:::warning
Do not use floating-point division naively in Java: `4 * 80 / 50` with integer
arithmetic truncates to 6, and you lose the ceil. Either compute
`(replicas * utilization + target - 1) / target` or convert to double and use
`Math.ceil` — then clamp to at least 1.
:::

:::tip
The HPA also has a `maxReplicas` cap — the formula proposes a number, and the
controller clamps it to `[1, maxReplicas]`. Here the upper clamp is your
problem, not the autoscaler's.
:::
