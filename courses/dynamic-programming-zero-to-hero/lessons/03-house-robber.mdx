      prev2 = prev1 = 0

      return prev1

Now each house offers a *choice*: rob it or skip it. DP problems are often just a
chain of "choose or not choose" decisions. This lesson teaches that decision.

## The decision at each house

Standing at house `i`, you either:

- **Rob it** — which forces you to skip house `i-1`. You get `nums[i]` plus the
  best you could do two houses back.
- **Skip it** — you keep whatever the best was at house `i-1`.

## The recurrence

```
dp[i] = max(dp[i-1], dp[i-2] + nums[i])
```

This is the single most reused recurrence in all of DP. It shows up in stock
problems, paint-house problems, and more.

:::warning
The most common bug is adding `nums[i]` to `dp[i-1]`. You cannot rob adjacent
houses, so robbing `i` means the previous house (`i-1`) must be skipped — you add
to `dp[i-2]`, not `dp[i-1]`.
:::

## Read it as a story

Every DP table is a story. Read `dp[i]` as "the best result for the first `i`
houses". Moving forward one house, you decide to include it or not, always
carrying the best-so-far. That is the essence of optimal substructure in decision
problems.

:::tip
You only need the last two values. Track `prev2` and `prev1`, and for each house
compute `cur = max(prev1, prev2 + x)`, then slide: `prev2 = prev1; prev1 = cur`.
:::
